Gaussian elimination

Follow the row operations that turn a matrix into echelon form—and see how the same operations produce its PALU decomposition.

From elimination to PALU

At each pivot, eliminate entries below it by subtracting a multiple of the pivot row. The multiples are recorded in a unit lower-triangular matrix $L$; the resulting echelon matrix is $U$. If the pivot rows are reordered, that row permutation is the permutation matrix $P$.

$$PA=LU$$

Here $P$ reorders rows of the original matrix, $L$ stores the elimination multipliers, and $U$ is the row-echelon result. For a square nonsingular matrix, this is the familiar PALU factorization. The same identity also applies to the rectangular matrices in this demo.

  1. Find the leftmost column with a nonzero entry among the rows not yet chosen.
  2. Choose a pivot row there. Partial pivoting chooses the largest absolute entry in that column.
  3. For each remaining row, record $\ell_{ik}=a_{i,j_k}/a_{k,j_k}$, where $j_k$ is the pivot column, then apply $R_i\leftarrow R_i-\ell_{ik}R_k$.
  4. Place the pivot row next in the final row order; repeat. Rows with no pivot go at the bottom.

A pivot is the first nonzero entry of a row after earlier elimination steps. A row-echelon matrix has pivots moving strictly right as you go down, with zero rows at the bottom.

The Jacobi method

Diagonalize a real symmetric matrix by following a sequence of plane rotations. Each step removes a symmetric pair of off-diagonal entries while preserving the eigenvalues.

From plane rotations to eigenvectors

The Jacobi method repeatedly chooses the largest off-diagonal entry and applies an orthogonal rotation in the corresponding coordinate plane. The transformed matrix stays symmetric, and its off-diagonal entries approach zero.

$$A_k=Q_k^T A_0 Q_k,\qquad A_0=Q_k A_k Q_k^T$$

The columns of $Q_k$ accumulate the rotations. When $A_k$ is nearly diagonal, its diagonal entries approximate the eigenvalues and the columns of $Q_k$ are the corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors.

  1. Find an off-diagonal entry $a_{pq}$ with largest absolute value.
  2. Choose a plane rotation that makes the $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$ entries zero.
  3. Apply the same orthogonal change of coordinates to the matrix and accumulate it in $Q$.
  4. Repeat until the off-diagonal part is small; read the eigenvalues from the diagonal.

The off-diagonal Frobenius norm is used as a convergence measure. Rotations preserve the Frobenius norm and symmetry.
